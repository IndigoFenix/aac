3 · Does it change the LABEL?

What actually reaches the Observer is a word, not a number. The mirrored frame must produce the mirrored label; anything else is an error the AI would see. Shown at the app's current threshold and at a less twitchy one.

mismatch% = flipped frame gave the wrong label. flips% = label changed between consecutive frames while your head was SETTLED — that is the "too sensitive" number, and it should be near 0. centre% = share of settled frames called "centre"; prod [SCENE] lines suggest this is very low in real sessions.
If mismatch is near 0 at both thresholds, the model asymmetry never reaches the AI and you can stop worrying about it. If flips% is high at 0.15 and much lower at 0.30, the problem is threshold sensitivity, not bias.

The two asym rows are the discriminator for the +1–2° you are seeing. Both are mean(residual)/mean(|yaw|), so both read as a % gain difference between your two sides and are directly comparable despite one being degrees and the other a ratio.
Both non-zero and similar → a real asymmetry in MediaPipe's landmarks.
Matrix non-zero, landmark ≈0 → the Euler extraction is leaking pitch; the model is fine and only yawDegFromMatrix needs correcting. The leak row confirms it: pitch is the one component a mirror does not flip, so a non-zero slope there is the leak's signature.

The three bins take settled frames only (head rate under 12°/s), so a timing skew cannot contribute to them — hold each pose briefly rather than sweeping continuously. Bins flat and ≈0 → estimator is clean, the asymmetry is you. Flat but ≠0 → offset bias. Growing with turn size → gain bias, i.e. one side really does read bigger.

Separately, τ is fitted from the moving frames: residual should equal τ·(yaw rate) if one pass trails the other, so the slope IS the lag in milliseconds. Near 0 means the two passes are aligned. Move your head to populate it.

  1. Raise only your RIGHT hand, palm to camera.
    → "hand seen" should read Right. If it says Left, the source stream is mirrored and every sided signal in the app is flipped.
  2. Wink your LEFT eye only.
    → eyeBlinkLeft should be the larger number. If eyeBlinkRight spikes instead, blendshape sides are image-relative, not subject-relative.
  3. Turn your head to your own RIGHT (~30°), keep eyes on the screen.
    → both yaw readings should go positive. The app's convention is +yaw = turned to the subject's right.
  4. Sit off to one side of the camera, facing it straight on.
    → watch yaw — app formula. If it drifts away from 0 while you are genuinely facing the camera, that is the nose-parallax bias, not a real head turn.
