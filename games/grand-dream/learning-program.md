# The Aivota Game-Based Learning System

The ultimate goal is for there to be no clear distinction between the AAC, the online socialization, and the game-based learning program. These are not distinct, separate systems - they are endpoints in a single system, growing independently until they merge together. Depending on the student's needs, any of these systems can be removed with the others remaining intact.

## The Universe Sandbox

A procedurally generated universe for teaching language, science, math, and social studies all at once. The player can interact with the world at different scales. Many of the "games" we are releasing in the apps menu are actually bits and pieces of this system that are gradually being linked together. (The Sandbox is the prototype for the geology section, Space Trader is the foundation for the trading puzzles)

Everything is being designed with scalable complexity, so it can be explored at an early age and grows alongside the student's ability to understand it.
Everything is also being designed with eyegaze controls in mind.

- Astronomy - players can watch planet formation and explore the solar system and galaxy
- Geology - a "sandbox" that lets players play with tectonic plates, creating mountains, which form rivers and ecosystems
- Ecology - simulate the interaction between different plants and animals that form around the mountains and rivers
- Society - villages show up around natural resources and grow into civilizations

## The Symbol Learning Program

This slides directly into the "Society" portion of the Universe Sandbox. Players can explore villages and interact with villagers, who communicate using a simplified, glyph-based system - the same system that is used by the AAC itself.

- Stage 1: Simple needs - Villagers request specific items ("I want"), which the player must acquire by making the same requests from shopkeepers using the AAC board, learning to ask for things they need.
- Stage 2: Trading and possession - The player must exchange items for other items in a bartering system. Teaches concepts like "I have", "I will give", "I will trade". "Me" and "You" become important here.
- Stage 3: Modification - Items have adjectives, and may need to be modified in order to fulfill needs. Players interact with objects in their surroundings to apply these changes to items (wet, dry, hot, cold)
- Stage 4: Asking Questions - Villagers might not say what they need right away, or where to find it - the player must learn to ask them to get clues.
- Stage 5: Cause and Effect - Concepts like "why" and "how" come into play as puzzles may involve multi-step processes.

## Mathematics and Reactive Growth

The game adjusts itself automatically based on the student's ability to master each earlier stage. Because its systems operate independently, and everything runs off of simplified language, it can serve as a learning tool that allows the student to grow at their own pace.

As trading become more complex, the player will learn applied math in order to manage resources. This system is flexible and focused on practical results within a realistic context: players are not "answering test questions", they are solving resource-management problems naturally in order to maximize their effectiveness within the game world. The game itself is the teaching tool.

Economics, statistics and social studies: The game world operates using realistic simulated economies, and the player will be able to explore these systems at their own pace to better understand high-level concepts. All of these concepts will have symbols associated with them.

## Socializing and the Social-Trainer

Characters within the world may use an LLM to interact socially with students. They have a programmed personality and students must learn how to interact with them - balancing asking questions with bids - in order to build rapport.
The game also has multiplayer interactions - players can enter the same world, explore together, and communicate through video chat.

# Customization

This system serves as the foundation for the game development portion of the Aivota CliniAACian platform. Each part of the game world operates independently of every other part, and because of its breadth, it is possible to construct a more focused game to teach specific subjects simply by removing the portions that aren't needed and modifying what is left.
