# Brainstorming for Axolotl Game

A virtual pet for the student to interact with and forms the second half of the AAC platform. It is a learning program designed to teach a variety of skills.

Game screen "above" the main screen, can scroll into view by pressing a button.

The game screen consists of a canvas displaying a fish tank with the axolotl avatar in it, where they can interact with it, perform routine activities, and play games. Clicking at the bottom should return to the main screen.

## Game Regulation & Limiting
Axolotl should "get tired" when playing games too much, adjust settings to manage playtime
- Standard: Aim for microsessions of 2-10 minutes, with at least an hour in between sessions.

Dynamic difficulty system: Each game should be evaluated for its learning opportunities and reward potential
- Track student interest, success rate, difficulty and notable behaviors
- Games that student requests often and scores highly should be treated as intrinsic rewards - allow playing only after they have completed more productive activities
- Games that student has difficulty with and train skills that align with their goals should be actively encouraged
- Occasionally attach in-app rewards to games (treasures, tank decorations). If student shows higher interest in games with rewards, attach rewards to more productive games to encourage them
- To avoid an antagonistic role, Axolotl should not be presented as deliberately creating these limitations; the monitor and script handles them based on student goals and needs.

## Game types
- Pet care (background activity)
  - Feeding
  - Decoration
  Picking up virtual items and moving them to destination, following requests for specific items

- Tag/Hide-and-Seek
  - Teaches usage of the platform itself, hand-eye coordination, and object permanence
  - Eye version (look at target) and hand version (touch target)
  - Adjustable difficulty sliders - speed, hiding difficulty
  - Only treated as a "game" for very young or low-functioning students

- Shape Matching
- Word Matching
- Math games
- Social games

## Creative activities

## Ludonarrative harmony
- Home activities vs Adventures
  - Axolotl lives in pool/tank, wants to climb a mountain
  - Pool mechanics allow creative expression (arranging decorations, feeding), while also presenting hidden cognitive tests
  - Younger students only have home activities, older students will have access to challenges
  - Challenges are treated as adventures
  - Climbing a mountain -> Progress on goals is presented as progress up the mountain
